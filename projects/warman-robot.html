Design & build competition

Warman Robot

A team-designed and manufactured robot developed within strict cost, size and performance constraints.

The challenge

The challenge involved picking up 3 irregularly sized balls from a 3 x 3 grid. The robot was then required to cross over a large seesaw and deposit them all in a required drop off zone. The size was restrained so that it must fit within a 400mm cube.

Design development

Our initial design allowed for the robot to drive directly over the balls, and then an elastic grid would be lowered over the balls, where they would then pass through. As we continued to develop the robot, small iterations were added to add structural stability as well as explore more in depth than our initial draft.

Manufacturing & testing

The design was manufactured using a combination of laser cut and 3D printed components. Laser cutting was preffered for the larger more structural parts, this saved a large amount of cost on filament. This provided more accurate profiles, while requiring smarter ways to connect and reinforce the structure. The parts were then assembled into the final design, allowing the manufactured system to be compared against the original CAD model and design requirements.

My contribution

I created the inital CAD model as well as designing the interlocking mechanisms, component sizing, and the gears of the device. My CAD model received recognition by the lecturor after the preliminary submission, it was chosen as one of the top 5 in no order of 100 teams, due to the level of detail added.


I also created the Arduino code for the robot as well. This involved how the robot would move in each of the directions, and ensured that the mechanical parts were all functioning correctly. Through the use of functions I ensured that the code was kept modular and tidy, this allowed for any parameters to be quickly edited to ensure that the robot was working as intended. This also meant that the code was easily readable to another person.

Outcome & learning

The design went through many iterations throughout the competition, although the main concept always revolved around using an elastic mesh to capture the balls. Each iteration allowed us to identify problems with the design and make improvements based on testing and feedback.

Throughout the project, I also learnt a lot about approaching a larger engineering project in a group environment. This included using meeting minutes to track progress, divide responsibilities and ensure each team member understood what they needed to complete.
